--- DESCRIPTION ---
This one began with an empty wall.

My room needed a painting. Instead of buying one, I decided to make it myself. I chose summer - golden fields, long evening light, and the people who have lived enough years to truly understand it.

Somewhere in the middle of that work, something happened that I didn't plan for. I looked at the elderly faces glowing in those amber fields, and I was moved to tears. Twice, actually - and I'm not someone who cries easily. But the golden years and the golden light of summer... they belong together in a way I can't fully explain. One is the reward for the other.

That one painting grew into a dream I've been quietly carrying ever since: an album called THE SEASONS. Four chapters, four seasons, one lifetime. This film is the first chapter - Summer.

I have a confession about the music. I wrote four different demos for this episode - four melodies, four sets of lyrics - and I spent weeks listening to them over and over, completely torn. Each one had its own beauty, and I was only allowed to keep one. Then one evening, "We Kept the Sun" came on while the light was fading outside my window, and I stopped searching. Some songs don't ask to be chosen. They just stay.

The other three songs won't appear in the next episodes. It feels a little like leaving good friends behind. But if life allows, Spring, Autumn and Winter are still ahead of us - the paintings take an unbelievable amount of time, and so does the music. I think you'd tell me the good things usually do.

This film is for everyone who has outlasted their share of storms and can still stand in the evening light, hold a familiar hand, and call it gold. You didn't just survive the fire. You kept the sun.

This scenic AI music video celebrates the beauty of summer through the eyes of those in their golden years - elderly couples in warm golden-hour light, amber wheat fields at harvest time, fireflies at dusk, porch coffee going cold, and a slow evening dance. "We Kept the Sun" is an original Americana folk-soul duet - weathered male and female voices, weeping cello and violin, fingerpicked guitar, and a gospel choir answering in the chorus. Slow, warm, and made to be replayed on a quiet evening.
